What is an Exoplanet?

An exoplanet is a planet that orbits a star outside our Solar System. The word "exoplanet" simply means extrasolar planet. Some exoplanets are similar in size to Earth, while others are much larger gas giants. They can also orbit their stars at very different distances.

Astronomers have discovered thousands of confirmed exoplanets. Most of them are extremely far away, so they cannot usually be seen directly with ordinary telescopes. Instead, scientists often detect them by observing how they affect the light or motion of their host star.

How are Exoplanets Discovered?

One of the most successful methods is the transit method. When a planet passes in front of its star, it blocks a very small amount of the star's light. By measuring this small decrease in brightness, astronomers can find the planet and estimate its size.

Another important method is the radial velocity method. A planet's gravity causes its star to move slightly as the planet orbits it. This motion produces small changes in the star's spectrum, which can be measured by astronomers.

Some Interesting Exoplanets

Exoplanet Interesting fact
51 Pegasi b Discovered in 1995, it was the first confirmed exoplanet found orbiting a Sun-like star. It is a hot Jupiter with a very short orbital period.
Proxima Centauri b It orbits Proxima Centauri, the closest known star to the Sun other than the Sun itself. It is roughly Earth-sized and receives an amount of stellar energy that places it in the star's habitable zone.
TRAPPIST-1e TRAPPIST-1e is one of seven known planets orbiting the small red dwarf star TRAPPIST-1. It is roughly Earth-sized and is one of the planets of particular interest for atmospheric studies.
Kepler-452b Kepler-452b orbits a star similar to the Sun and takes about 385 days to complete one orbit. It is larger than Earth and is located in the star's habitable zone.
WASP-12b WASP-12b is a very hot gas giant that orbits extremely close to its host star. It completes an orbit in only about one day and has an atmosphere strongly affected by the intense heat.

Why are Exoplanets Important?

The study of exoplanets helps astronomers understand how planets form and evolve around different types of stars. Our Solar System contains only a small number of planets, so studying planetary systems around other stars gives scientists a much larger sample to compare with.

One of the major questions in exoplanet research is whether some of these worlds could have conditions suitable for life. Scientists look at factors such as planetary size, temperature, distance from the star and the possible presence of an atmosphere. Finding a planet in a habitable zone does not mean that life has been found, but it can make the planet interesting for further study.
