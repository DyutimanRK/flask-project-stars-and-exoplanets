What is a Star?

A star is a huge ball of extremely hot plasma held together by its own gravity. Most stars are mainly made of hydrogen and helium. Deep inside their cores, nuclear fusion converts hydrogen into helium and releases enormous amounts of energy as light and heat. The colour, temperature, size and lifetime of a star depend mainly on its mass and stage of evolution.

Some Famous Stars

Stars can be very different from one another. Some are relatively small and cool, while others are enormous and extremely hot. Here are a few well-known stars that are interesting to study.

Star Picture Type Some Information
Sun G-type Main Sequence Star The Sun is the closest star to Earth and is the centre of our Solar System. It provides the light and energy required for life on Earth.
Sirius A-type Main Sequence Star Sirius is the brightest star in Earth's night sky. It is actually a binary star system containing Sirius A and its much fainter companion, Sirius B.
Betelgeuse Red Supergiant Betelgeuse is a huge red supergiant located in the constellation Orion. It is in a late stage of stellar evolution and is expected to eventually end its life as a supernova.
Vega A-type Main Sequence Star Vega is one of the brightest stars in the northern sky and is located about 25 light-years from Earth in the constellation Lyra.
Polaris Yellow Supergiant Polaris is commonly called the North Star because it lies very close to the direction of the Earth's north celestial pole. It is part of a multiple-star system.

Different Types of Stars

Stars are classified according to properties such as their temperature, colour and spectral characteristics. Main-sequence stars like the Sun produce energy by fusing hydrogen in their cores. More massive stars can become red supergiants, while smaller stars can eventually become white dwarfs after passing through later stages of evolution.

A Sky Full of Stars

When we look at the night sky, the stars may appear to be small points of light, but they are actually enormous objects located at very different distances from Earth. The patterns we see form constellations, which have been used by different cultures for navigation and storytelling for thousands of years.
